Common Grounds Café

Section 01
Brand Overview

A quick snapshot of Common Grounds Café before strategy implementation.

This portfolio is deliberately lighter than a full Authority build — no competitor analysis, funnel mapping, or CTA optimization. Those are Momentum and Authority-tier features. What's below is exactly what a Foundation client receives: audit, basic strategy, a calendar, and the content itself.
Business Type
Independent neighborhood café and casual workspace
Description
A cozy café known for specialty coffee, fresh pastries, and seating that welcomes people who want to stay a while
Target Audience
Local remote workers, students, weekend brunch-goers, and neighborhood regulars within walking distance
Brand Positioning
The café that feels like your living room, not a waiting room
Brand Voice
Warm, casual, a little witty — never corporate
Audience Pain Point
Tired of overpriced chain coffee with no personality, wants a calm space to work without feeling rushed out

Full Audit Notes

Bio just lists the café name and city — no hours, no positioning, no link to order or find them

No Story activity, so daily specials and weekend events go almost entirely unseen

Section 03
Audience Research

Strategic audience segmentation behind the Common Grounds Café content system.

The remote worker
25–35. Needs reliable wifi and a quiet corner for the morning. Checks Instagram before showing up to gauge how busy it looks.
The weekend regular
28–45. Comes for Saturday brunch with friends or family. Picks where to go based on what looks worth the wait on Instagram.
The student
18–24. Budget-conscious, looking for a study spot with good music. Heavily influenced by what friends repost or tag them in.
Section 04
Content Strategy

The content pillars driving Common Grounds Café's strategy.

The menu
Drinks, food, seasonal specials — the pillar that makes people think "I want that."
The vibe
Atmosphere, seating, lighting, music — the pillar that makes people think "I want to be there."
The community
Regulars, staff, local events — the pillar that turns first-timers into people who come back weekly.
